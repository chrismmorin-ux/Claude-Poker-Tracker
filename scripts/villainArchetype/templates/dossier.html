How to read the score

Coverage is {{COVERAGE}}% — but that is true by construction, not an achievement. Every decision falls into some leaf, so coverage can never be anything else. It is not evidence.

What carries information is rule count. {{RULES}} rules reproduce {{DECISIONS}} decisions at {{ACCURACY}}% accuracy; that ratio is the compression, and compression is the claim. Accuracy on its own is also not evidence — a rule that says "when the price is bad, he folds" is right nine times in ten because folding is what mostly happens.

So the honest read is: {{RULES}} statements reproduce this player. The rest of this page is about where those {{RULES}} stop working.

The ruleset, in his own voice

Each rule carries the number of decisions it covers, the split between his choices, and a 95% confidence interval on each. A rule is not a prediction — it is a description of a spot and what he does in it.

{{RULECARDS}}

What his range can make

A frequency on its own cannot be read. Betting 16% is what a nit does with a narrow value range and what a maniac does with half his hands, and the two need opposite responses. This is what his range actually makes on the boards he faced — so every frequency above has a ceiling to be read against.

{{STRENGTH}}

Exact rows are the big blind in a limped pot: continuing was free, so his range genuinely is all 1,326 hands and these numbers depend on nothing but the board. Assumed rows use his measured entry width with composition filled by an equity ordering. They are never pooled.

Where he bets less often than he can hold top pair or better, he is checking value. Where he bets more often than value and draws combined, the excess is bluff by arithmetic — no card has to be shown for that to follow.

Reading the ladder

Overlapping intervals mean the step between two neighbouring seats is not separately established, even when the ordering across the whole ladder is. Where two rows share a seat name at different table sizes, they are different spots — a different field sits behind him — and they are listed separately for that reason.

His ranges

Two different claims sit on every grid below, and they have completely different evidence behind them.

Width is measured. It comes from every decision he took in the spot, cards or no cards. A player who enters 12.5% of pots refutes an 8% range outright, with no card ever shown.

Composition is assumed. The only hard constraints are the hands he actually turned face up. Everything else is filled in by imposing a strength ordering — hand classes sorted by equity against an opening range — until the measured width is reached. That ordering is our convention. It is not an observation about him.

Which is why each grid carries its own falsifier: any hand he showed that the ordering puts outside the range refutes the ordering for that spot. That happened on {{REFUTED}} of {{CHARTCOUNT}} grids.

The refutations are the most informative rows. A hand he was caught holding that the assumed strength ordering places outside the range is a hand he demonstrably plays and our ordering excludes — which refutes the ordering for that spot, not the width.

What we still cannot see

His cards are known on {{SHOWNTOTAL}} of {{DECISIONS}} decisions — {{CARDPCT}}%. On the remaining {{BLINDPCT}}% we know the situation and the action and nothing about the holding.

Worse, the {{CARDPCT}}% is not a random sample. A hand reaches showdown by continuing, so strong hands are over-represented among the ones we see. That cuts one way and it is useful: seeing him turn over aces tells us almost nothing, because the filter produces that anyway. Seeing a small pair or a suited connector survive that filter is evidence running against the bias — which is exactly why the refutations above are load-bearing and the confirmations mostly are not.

The shown hands are a floor on how wide his ranges are. They are never the range.

What his folds prove

Cards are visible on a small, survivor-filtered slice of his decisions. The action that ENDS a hand is visible on all of them — and it is a statement about what he did not hold. When the draw completes and he folds to a bet, he does not have it. Not probably: the combinations are enumerable and they are gone.

{{TERMINAL}}

These three are deliberately never pooled, because they are different kinds of claim. An exclusion is a fact about the fold and assumes nothing about him. A prior is a read about him, only as good as the showdowns behind it, which is why its count travels with it. The contrast assumes nothing at all — it is two frequencies split on an event that happened on the table, which makes it the cleanest evidence on this page.

What this instrument could not have found

Every other measurement here asks what he did. This one asks what the machinery is blind to, and it is the only question whose answer does not depend on him at all. A dossier that reports no timing tell, no tilt and no opponent-specific adjustment reads as a description of a stable player — and would read exactly the same if the record simply had no timestamps, no session marks and no opponent ids. Those are opposite in meaning and identical on the page.
