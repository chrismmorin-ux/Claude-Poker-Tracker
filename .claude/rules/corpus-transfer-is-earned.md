# Corpus transfer is EARNED, per quantity, by test

**Founder ruling, 2026-08-20.** Resolves ~31 open queue items that each carried "corpus vs
founder's own hands" as a per-item decision flag. It replaces a global answer with a test.

> *"The amount that proves itself to be the same as modern villains."*

## The rule

**No quantity crosses from the corpus into a live claim by default, and none is forbidden by
default. Each one crosses when it is shown to hold against modern data, and it carries that
showing.**

The question is never "does the corpus transfer" — that has no answer. It is always **"does
*this* quantity transfer, and what showed it."**

This replaces the framing the fault register currently uses, where every corpus-anchored claim
is uniformly "transferred, not measured." That framing is right about levels and wrong about
method, and the difference is exactly what this rule makes expressible.

## Three things the corpus produces, and they do not have the same standing

1. **Method / primitives — how to identify an exploitable characteristic and seize the spot.**
   The corpus villains were real people who really did those things, so the *pattern* is real.
   This is what the corpus is primarily for. It carries the lowest transfer burden.
2. **Structure — which features separate players, and the shape of a relationship.** Transfers
   only with a test. Shape is an empirical claim about a population like any other, and it can
   be population-specific.
3. **Levels — rates, frequencies, the numbers themselves.** The highest burden. A 2009 online
   fold rate reported about a live 1/2 table is the top-ranked entry in the Suspected-Fault
   Register for a reason.

## The three-object separation this ruling establishes

- **The corpus** teaches how to identify and exploit. Its output is primitives.
- **Modern data** identifies the subject — the villain who is actually in today's pool.
- **Simulation** covers what is not yet known about a live villain mid-session, because *"there
  is still something you don't know yet about someone you are playing with and you're gathering
  information."*

Simulation is not a substitute for data here; it is how the unknown remainder is represented
while it is still unknown. It must be able to **recreate an individual** — a synthetic player
generating actions "in primitive order by action and hand, realistically" — which is precisely
why a Conduct Card cannot be sampled from directly (WS-580: a marginal policy makes every villain
play every holding identically, and that is simulating a frequency table, not poker).

## Applying it

1. Name the quantity and which of the three kinds it is.
2. State what test would show it holds on modern data, and the n that test needs.
3. Until that test runs, the quantity is **usable and labelled**, or **refused** — decided by
   `.claude/rules/sparsity-refuse-or-shrink.md`: comparative claim → refuse; internal decision
   input → usable, shrunk, and carrying the label.
4. When the test runs, its result goes on the quantity permanently. A transfer that has been
   earned stops being a caveat and becomes a measurement.
5. **Never report a corpus level about a live table without the transfer status on its face.**

## The trap, named

Validating a simulator against data generated by the same model shrinks the interval around a
fixed bias and proves nothing. The valid forms are the calibration null, power analysis, held-out
both ways, the synthetic round-trip (WS-583), and — strongest under the cold-read regime —
optimal experimental design to choose the discriminating question.
