## Why this exists

Every number this repo could previously produce about model quality scores **villain
action prediction** — 58.7% accuracy, 0.758 log-loss (WS-285). That is an *input* to good
advice, not evidence of it. Between a correct villain model and a correct recommendation
sits the whole POKER_THEORY §6.1–6.4 spine — value thresholds, bluff breakeven, MDF,
sizing, equity realization, multiway adjustment — and none of it had ever been scored.
The 2026-07-26 synthesis put it first on the list of what was never looked at:

> **Hero's side. We never measured whether the ADVICE is good.** … A perfectly calibrated
> villain model behind a broken game tree still loses money.

## The two constraints that shaped the design

**1. The corpus has no hero hole cards.** PHH masks them (`d dh p1 ????`); only showdown
reveals exist. But `evaluateGameTree` *requires* `heroCards`. Scoring only showdown-revealed
hands is not an option — a player who folds never shows, so that subset contains **zero
fold decisions** and conditions on reaching showdown, an event downstream of the decision
being scored.

Resolved by treating the advice as a **distribution over actions**, taken over the holdings
hero could hold at that node. The app already infers that range; the seam now emits it
(`rangeBefore`, captured *before* hero's own action is folded in, so the advice is not
conditioned on the action being scored). No hole cards needed, no showdown selection.

**2. Scoring with the engine's own EV arithmetic proves nothing.** The engine picks the
argmax-EV action under its own model, so grading it with that arithmetic is near-tautological.
Value therefore comes from **realized corpus chips**, reconstructed from the action sequence,
board and shown cards.

*Founder decisions, 2026-07-28:* per-decision importance sampling on real outcomes; and
certify *structure*, stress-testing the *value* with a live-shifted sensitivity band.

**Units, precisely.** The outcome attached to a decision is the whole HAND's net for hero,
so this is expected hand value evaluated at the decision level — *not* a per-decision
increment and *not* a winrate. A hand with a 30bb swing contributes ±30 to every decision
it contains. **+12 bb here is not "12bb per decision of profit."**

### C3: FAIL — and that is a real result, not a bug

The bar is a positive edge whose 95% CI excludes zero, in both the corpus and live-shifted
arms. The corpus CI is **[−7.60, +22.17]**. It straddles zero, so the honest reading is:

> **At this sample, the engine's advice is not demonstrably better than population-typical
> play. The point estimate is favourable and the interval does not support it.**

Recorded to `scorecard-history.yaml` regardless of being unfavourable, per the accept
criteria. C3 now reads `edge 12.042, CI low -7.597 (must exceed 0)` instead of
`NO INSTRUMENT`.

### Three things that say the instrument is sound

1. **The control is exactly zero.** Scoring the behaviour policy against itself gives
   `0.000` with a zero-width interval, on real data. If importance weighting were
   manufacturing signal, this is where it would show, and it does not.
2. **The passive baseline is significantly NEGATIVE.** Always folding / always checking
   scores −7.56 bb with CI [−14.37, −0.46] — excluding zero. So the instrument *can*
   resolve an effect at this sample size when the effect is large. It is not merely
   noise-blind.
3. **Weights are well behaved.** Mean weight 1.07, **0.0% clipped** at the cap of 20. The
   result is not being propped up by a handful of extreme weights.

### The outcome distribution is genuinely heavy-tailed

Scored decisions carry hand nets from −69.5 bb to **+369.2 bb** (quartiles −6 / −3 / +4.7).
The tail was checked rather than assumed: ~0.9% of corpus pots exceed 200 bb, the largest
sampled being a 511 bb pot in which two seats each committed 127.5 chips at 0.5 bb — deep
stacks, ledger balancing to zero. **This is real poker, not a reconstruction artifact.**

It is also a permanent precision constraint. It is the same heavy tail that made the
2026-07-26 fold-regret bb/100 figure unquotable ("usually free, occasionally very
expensive"), and it means a wide interval here is the data being honest, not the estimator
being weak.

One selection effect worth naming: the pool's mean net across scored decisions is **+5.23
bb**, not ~0, because a scored decision requires the player to have voluntarily reached
postflop — which selects hands that were already going well. This cancels in the *edge*
(both arms are scored on the same decisions) but it means the arm *levels* are not
winrates.

### The binding constraint is PLAYER-CLUSTERS, not decisions

**9 player-clusters** produced the 250 scored decisions. The CI is a cluster bootstrap over
players — the level at which the corpus is genuinely independent — so the interval is
essentially being driven by nine exchangeable units. Effective sample size is 54.7 (21.9%
of n), which is the same fact from the weighting side.

The concentration is severe: three players supply 182 of the 250 decisions (73%), with the
remainder spread across six players contributing as few as three each.

**More decisions per player will not fix this.** Narrowing the interval needs *more distinct
EVAL players*, which means a much wider corpus scan (this run read 60 of 1,756 matched
files) and correspondingly more compute.

**Measured cost:** ~4.2 s per scored decision end-to-end (~1,058 s for 250), dominated by
per-call fixed work in `evaluateGameTree` rather than Monte Carlo trials — `--combo-samples`
is the real lever, not `--trials`.

## Named limitations of v1 — stated here, not discovered later

- **POSTFLOP ONLY.** The `accumulateDecisions` seam skips preflop by design (preflop is
  handled by aggregate stats). The preflop half of the spine is unscored.
- **Sizing is not scored.** Actions collapse to primitives (`fold/check/call/bet/raise`)
  because corpus labels are primitives. Sizing selection *is* part of the spine; this
  instrument does not test it.
- **One-decision horizon.** Multi-street consequences of our advice are not measured.
- **Villain held at POPULATION baseline.** Personalization is already scored by C1; holding
  the villain flat isolates the decision spine rather than re-measuring the villain model
  through it. The interaction of the two is a v2 arm.
- **Hero equity is model-derived.** The realized *outcome* is observed; the *range*
  attributed to hero is the engine's own inference.
- **Rake is MODELLED** (5%, $3 cap, no-flop-no-drop). The corpus records none. Both raked
  and unraked arms are always reported; here the edge does not depend on rake.
- **The live-shifted arm re-weights 2009 outcomes.** It answers "if the field folded more,
  would this advice still look good on these hands" — it is not a live simulation.
- **π_ours resolution is 1/K.** At `--combo-samples 8`, the finest resolvable probability
  is 0.125.

### Two defects found and fixed on the way

1. **The bootstrap RNG had a low-bit trap.** `bootstrapMeanCI` drew indices with
   `state % n` from a power-of-two-modulus LCG, whose low bits cycle with period as short
   as the modulus. At 4 clusters it returned `0,1,2,3` forever — every "resample" drew one
   of each cluster, and the interval **collapsed to zero width**: a CI that reads as
   perfect precision for the worst possible reason. Now draws from the high bits. This
   also affected the shipped `evCost.mjs` CI quoted in the 2026-07-26 baseline doc.
2. **`--record` never existed.** `model-readiness.mjs` documented the flag in its usage
   header and imported `appendFileSync`, but `main()` had no branch for it. Implemented,
   with metrics carried forward from the previous row (a hero-EV-only row would otherwise
   leave accuracy null and fail C1, reading as a regression when nothing regressed) and
   carried values marked as inherited.

## How to reproduce

```bash
# 1. Mine the behaviour policy from the POOL half (once per split)
node scripts/backtest/mine-behavior-policy.mjs \
  --stakes 50NLH --max-players 400 --max-hands-per-player 150 \
  --out out/behavior-policy.json

# 2. Score EVAL hero decisions against realized chips
node scripts/backtest/run-hero-ev.mjs \
  --reference none --behavior-policy out/behavior-policy.json \
  --stakes 50NLH --max-files 60 --max-players 80 \
  --max-hands-per-player 250 --max-decisions 250 \
  --combo-samples 8 --trials 150 --out out/hero-ev-smoke.json

# 3. Record and re-evaluate the gate
node scripts/readiness/model-readiness.mjs --record --from out/hero-ev-smoke.json \
  --label "…" --arm "hero-ev"
node scripts/readiness/model-readiness.mjs
```

Tests: `npx vitest run scripts/__tests__/heroEv.test.js scripts/__tests__/heroEvPolicy.test.js`
(57 tests). The load-bearing ones are the identities: zero-sum chip accounting, and
π_ours = π_pool ⟹ edge exactly zero.

## What would move C3

In descending order of expected effect:

1. **More EVAL player-clusters.** The interval is bounded by 9 clusters, not 250 decisions.
   A run over the full 1,756-file scan is the direct fix and is a multi-hour batch job at
   the measured ~4.2 s/decision.
2. **Score sizing.** The engine's sizing choice is currently invisible to the instrument,
   and sizing is where a decision spine most plausibly adds or destroys value.
3. **Extend to preflop**, which needs a seam that does not skip preflop entries.
4. **Turn the villain model back on** as a second arm, to measure the spine and the
   personalization together rather than in isolation.
