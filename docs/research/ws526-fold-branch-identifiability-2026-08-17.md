| | |

| Pre-registered tolerance | 0.02 at N = 2,000,000 |
| **Measured at headline N** | **0.00309** |

The claim under test was the design record's §5.2 argument — that π(a | s, h) is identifiable
including its fold branch even though folding hides the holding permanently. **It survived.**

**And the default hypothesis is refuted, unhedged: it is not true that a hand which never
reveals tells you nothing about which holding folded.** Range conservation carries the
information. The folded mass at a node is the parent range minus the continuing branches, and
the continuing branches are anchored by showdown.

## §4 THE BOUNDARY: identification requires hand-class MIGRATION

This was not anticipated and it is the most consequential output of the run.

Setting the class-transition matrix to the identity — hand strength never changes across
streets — **destroys identification entirely**:

| Transition | smallest Hessian eigenvalue | Hands to resolve a 0.05 error along the flattest direction |
|---|---|---|
| Realistic (off-diagonal mass 0.283) | 2.98 × 10⁻² | **51,635** |
| **Identity (no migration)** | **−1.26 × 10⁻³, never stabilises across the step ladder** | **INFINITE — not identified** |

The migration sweep turns the boundary into a dial:

| Mix | Off-diagonal mass | λ_min | Hands to resolve |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0.000 | −1.26 × 10⁻³ (singular) | **INFINITE** |
| 0.10 | 0.028 | 3.99 × 10⁻³ | 385,387 |
| 0.25 | 0.071 | 9.65 × 10⁻³ | 159,235 |
| 0.50 | 0.142 | 1.77 × 10⁻² | 86,790 |
| 0.75 | 0.213 | 2.41 × 10⁻² | 63,741 |
| 1.00 | 0.283 | 2.98 × 10⁻² | 51,635 |

**Why it happens.** When class never changes, a hand's whole action sequence is generated by
one class, and the streets stop being separable — a coordinated shift across streets and
classes leaves the likelihood essentially unchanged. When class migrates, each street's action
is generated by a *different* draw, and the cross-street structure pins the parameters.

**This is good news for real poker and it is not a free pass.** Board runouts genuinely move
hand strength, so the real game sits toward the right of that table. But *how far* right is a
measurable property of the corpus, and it bounds everything downstream.

> **BINDING ON WS-527: measure the corpus class-transition matrix and compute λ_min BEFORE
> fitting.** A fit on a near-singular geometry returns a confident number that no sample size
> supports, and nothing else in the pipeline would catch it.

## §5 Two instruments were wrong, and one nearly shipped as the headline

Relayed prominently because both were mine and both looked right.

### 5.1 Axis-aligned probing understates by 10× to infinity

`resolvability` perturbs one policy cell at a time. On the realistic case it reported **4,895**
hands where the true answer along the flattest *joint* direction is **51,635** — understating by
**10.5×**. On the identity case it reported **6,547** hands for a direction that is *infinitely*
unresolvable.

**A flat direction in parameter space need not line up with any parameter.** The instrument that
tests one coordinate at a time cannot see it. `curvature` — the smallest eigenvalue of the
Hessian — does, whatever the orientation. It is pinned by a test that asserts the axis-aligned
version fails, so this cannot quietly come back.

### 5.2 The optimiser stalled and reported the stall as an answer

EM converged on **log-likelihood** change. On a near-flat direction that criterion declares
victory while the parameters are still wrong: at one point the fit sat **0.079** away from the
truth at a likelihood cost of **2.2 × 10⁻¹⁰**. The run reported `iterations: 5000` out of
`maxIter: 5000` with no flag, and the stall was found only by hand.

Fixed by converging on **parameter** change and returning `converged: false` on a stall. The
effect on the headline was not cosmetic:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden exact, max fold error | 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ | **1.6 × 10⁻¹⁰** |
| 200 random truths, worst | 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ | **1.1 × 10⁻⁸** |

### 5.3 The curvature instrument itself produced a false non-identification

Caught while writing this up. At the default finite-difference step, the K = 5 case reported
λ_min = **−5.4 × 10⁻³ → NOT IDENTIFIED**, while its EM recovery error was 3.8 × 10⁻¹¹. Both
cannot be true.

Stepping the finite difference:

| eps | λ_min (K = 5) |
|---|---|
| 1 × 10⁻³ | −7.17 |
| 1 × 10⁻⁴ | −5.4 × 10⁻³ |
| 1 × 10⁻⁵ | **+2.13 × 10⁻²** |

It was **numerical error**. K = 5 is identified. The instrument now refines the step until
λ_min stops moving and returns `stable: false` rather than a quotable number when it never
does — because **a false non-identification is the worst error this instrument can make: it
kills work that would have succeeded.**

Validated against a known answer: the oracle case returns λ_min = 3.6 × 10⁻¹ stable to three
digits across every step size.

## §6 Scaling with hand-class count

| Classes | Free parameters | Observation types | λ_min | Hands to resolve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 18 | 223 | 2.98 × 10⁻² | 51,635 |
| 5 | 30 | 1,007 | 2.33 × 10⁻² | 65,972 |
| 8 | 48 | 4,103 | 2.38 × 10⁻² | 64,583 |

**Hands-to-resolve stayed flat as parameters rose from 18 to 48.** Parameter count is not the
driver; the geometry is.

**Do not read 52k–66k as the answer for real poker.** This is 3 streets, one decision node per
street, no position, no sizing, no SPR, and up to 8 classes. Real state is far larger and the
number will grow. What the table supports is narrower and still useful: *adding hand classes
did not blow up the requirement.* The K = 5 and K = 8 transitions were randomly drawn rather
than matched, so this is a scaling indication, not a controlled comparison.

## §7 What this licenses, and what it does not

**Licensed.** WS-527 is funded. The mechanism it rests on is real, measured against a known
answer, and survives every control and stress case run here.

**Not licensed:**

1. **Any claim about the real corpus.** This is synthetic. The generator is a 3-class, 3-street
   abstraction, and the estimator is *given* the exact prior and transition — mirroring "P(h) at
   the root is exact combinatorics", but an assumption nonetheless, and one the wrong-T control
   shows is load-bearing.
2. **Any claim about live 9-handed 1/2–1/3.** Nothing here touches a real population.
3. **A hands-to-resolve figure for a real villain.** §6 says the number will grow; it does not
   say to what.

## §8 On the Result Card — a conflict I am flagging rather than resolving

WS-526's accept criteria (which **I wrote**, before reading the register closely) require a
Result Card. `VOCABULARY.md` sets a directly contrary precedent for the **Compression claim**:

> *"Deliberately NOT a Result Card: a Match is `Surface x Deal Book x Field`, and this claim has
> no surface, no strategy and no opponent population — it is a property of the deck. Minting a
> Card would mean inventing a `fieldId` for a measurement that has no field."*

By exact analogy, WS-526's claim has no surface, no strategy and no opponent population — **it
is a property of an estimator on synthetic data.** Minting a card would mean inventing a
`fieldId` and a `dealBookId` for a measurement that has neither.

I have not silently amended the accept criteria, because doing so after seeing a passing result
is indistinguishable from moving the goalposts. **This is the founder's call.** WS-527's corpus
fit unambiguously needs a Result Card — it has a real Deal Book and a real Field.
